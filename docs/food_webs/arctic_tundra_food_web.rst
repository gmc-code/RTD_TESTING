=======================
Arctic Tundra Food Web
=======================

The Arctic tundra is a cold, treeless plain near the North Pole. The ground
under the surface stays frozen all year, so only low plants and lichens can
grow. For a few weeks each summer the sun hardly sets, and the tundra bursts
into life. Willows, cotton grass and flowers feed lemmings, hares,
ptarmigan, caribou and muskoxen, and those feed owls, foxes and wolves.

A food web joins all the food chains in a community together. Each arrow
points from the food to the organism that eats it, so the arrows show which
way the energy goes.

Build the food web
==================

The four producers are already in place. Drag each animal from the tray into
the right box, then press **Check**. Tap a picture (or start dragging it) and
the box at the top of the tray says what that animal eats. The arrows are
clues too: look at what goes into and out of each box.

**Explore** shows the finished web: point at (or tap) an organism to see what
it eats and what eats it. **Trace a chain** builds a food chain by tapping the
organisms in order.

Food chains
===========

There are **17 food chains** in this web. Every chain has three organisms.

Starting at reindeer lichen (1)
-------------------------------

#. Reindeer lichen → Caribou → Arctic wolf

Starting at Arctic cotton grass (4)
-----------------------------------

#. Arctic cotton grass → Caribou → Arctic wolf
#. Arctic cotton grass → Muskox → Arctic wolf
#. Arctic cotton grass → Brown lemming → Arctic fox
#. Arctic cotton grass → Brown lemming → Snowy owl

Starting at Arctic willow (7)
-----------------------------

#. Arctic willow → Caribou → Arctic wolf
#. Arctic willow → Muskox → Arctic wolf
#. Arctic willow → Arctic hare → Arctic wolf
#. Arctic willow → Arctic hare → Arctic fox
#. Arctic willow → Arctic hare → Snowy owl
#. Arctic willow → Rock ptarmigan → Arctic fox
#. Arctic willow → Rock ptarmigan → Snowy owl

Starting at purple saxifrage (5)
--------------------------------

#. Purple saxifrage → Arctic hare → Arctic wolf
#. Purple saxifrage → Arctic hare → Arctic fox
#. Purple saxifrage → Arctic hare → Snowy owl
#. Purple saxifrage → Rock ptarmigan → Arctic fox
#. Purple saxifrage → Rock ptarmigan → Snowy owl

Checking the feeding relationships
==================================

Every link in the food web was checked against the sources listed at the
bottom.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 30 50

   * - Organism
     - Eats (in this web)
     - What the sources say
   * - Caribou
     - Reindeer lichen, Arctic cotton grass, Arctic willow
     - Lichen is the main winter food. In summer caribou eat willow leaves,
       and cotton grass flower heads are a favourite food in spring.
   * - Muskox
     - Arctic cotton grass, Arctic willow
     - Sedges (including cotton grass) and Arctic willow make up most of
       the diet.
   * - Brown lemming
     - Arctic cotton grass
     - Eats fresh grasses and sedges, which make up most of the summer diet.
       Cotton grass is a sedge.
   * - Arctic hare
     - Arctic willow, purple saxifrage
     - Arctic willow is the main food all year. It also eats the young
       flowers of purple saxifrage.
   * - Rock ptarmigan
     - Arctic willow, purple saxifrage
     - Eats willow buds and catkins, and scratches through shallow snow to
       reach purple saxifrage.
   * - Arctic wolf
     - Caribou, muskox, Arctic hare
     - Muskoxen and Arctic hares are the main prey. Wolves also eat caribou.
   * - Arctic fox
     - Brown lemming, Arctic hare, rock ptarmigan
     - Feeds mainly on lemmings, and also eats ptarmigan and young Arctic
       hares.
   * - Snowy owl
     - Brown lemming, Arctic hare, rock ptarmigan
     - Lemmings are by far the main food. Snowy owls also catch ptarmigan
       and young Arctic hares.

.. note::

   Some links are left out to keep the web readable. Lemmings and hares
   also eat mosses and lichens, ptarmigan chicks eat insects, wolves also
   eat lemmings, ptarmigan and Arctic foxes, and foxes and owls also take
   birds' eggs and voles. Decomposers and scavenging are left out of this
   web.

.. note::

   Reindeer lichen is not a plant. A lichen is a fungus and an alga living
   together. The alga makes food by photosynthesis, so the lichen is still a
   producer.
