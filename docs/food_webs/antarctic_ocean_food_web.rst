===========================
Antarctic Ocean Food Web
===========================

The Southern Ocean around Antarctica is one of the coldest seas on Earth, but
in summer it is full of life. Long hours of sunlight make tiny drifting
plants called phytoplankton grow in huge numbers. They feed swarms of krill
and other zooplankton, and those feed fish, squid, penguins, seals and
whales.

A food web joins all the food chains in a community together. Each arrow
points from the food to the organism that eats it, so the arrows show which
way the energy goes.

Build the food web
==================

The producer, phytoplankton, is already in place. Drag each animal from the
tray into the right box, then press **Check**. Tap a picture (or start
dragging it) and the box at the top of the tray says what that animal eats.
The arrows are clues too: look at what goes into and out of each box.

**Explore** shows the finished web: point at (or tap) an organism to see what
it eats and what eats it. **Trace a chain** builds a food chain by tapping the
organisms in order.

Food chains
===========

and ends at the killer whale. The shortest chains have four organisms and the
longest have six.

Checking the feeding relationships
==================================

Every link in the food web was checked against the sources listed at the
bottom.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 30 50

   * - Organism
     - Eats (in this web)
     - What the sources say
   * - Zooplankton
     - Phytoplankton
     - Tiny drifting animals such as copepods. Many of them graze on
       phytoplankton.
   * - Antarctic krill
     - Phytoplankton
     - Krill are built for filtering phytoplankton, especially diatoms. They
       also eat copepods (zooplankton), but that link is left out to keep the
       web simple.
   * - Antarctic icefish
     - Antarctic krill, zooplankton
     - Studies of mackerel icefish found krill is its preferred food. It also
       eats amphipods and mysids, which are zooplankton.
   * - Blue whale
     - Antarctic krill
     - Feeds almost entirely on krill, eating up to about four tonnes a day.
   * - Squid
     - Antarctic krill, zooplankton, Antarctic icefish
     - Antarctic squid feed on krill and small fish. Some species also eat
       amphipods (zooplankton).
   * - Adélie penguin
     - Antarctic krill, Antarctic icefish
     - Krill is the main food. Fish are also important, especially further
       south. Sources name silverfish and lanternfish rather than icefish, so
       the icefish card stands for "small fish" here.
   * - Leopard seal
     - Antarctic krill, Antarctic icefish, squid, Adélie penguin
     - A broad diet: krill, fish, squid, penguins and other seals. In one
       study krill, fish and penguins were each about a quarter to a third of
       the spring diet.
   * - Killer whale
     - Leopard seal, Adélie penguin, blue whale, squid, Antarctic icefish
     - Antarctic killer whales eat fish, squid, Adélie and emperor penguins,
       seals including leopard seals, and whales, including blue whales.

.. note::

   Some links are left out to keep the web readable. Krill also eat
   zooplankton, Adélie penguins sometimes eat squid, and leopard seals and
   killer whales also eat other seals that are not in this web.
