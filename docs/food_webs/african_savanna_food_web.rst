=========================
African Savanna Food Web
=========================

The African savanna is a warm grassland with scattered trees. It has a long
dry season and a wet season. Grasses and acacia trees are the producers, and
they feed some of the largest herds of plant-eating animals on Earth. Those
herds feed the big hunters.

A food web joins all the food chains in a community together. Each arrow
points from the food to the organism that eats it, so the arrows show which
way the energy goes.

Build the food web
==================

The three producers are already in place. Drag each animal from the tray
into the right box, then press **Check**. Tap a picture (or start dragging
it) and the box at the top of the tray says what that animal eats. The
arrows are clues too: look at what goes into and out of each box.

**Explore** shows the finished web: point at (or tap) an organism to see what
it eats and what eats it. **Trace a chain** builds a food chain by tapping the
organisms in order.

Checking the feeding relationships
==================================

Each card in the cut-out pack says what the organism eats. Every link in the
food web was checked against the sources listed at the bottom.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 28 54

   * - Organism
     - Card says it eats
     - What the sources say
   * - Blue wildebeest
     - Short grasses and shoots
     - A grazer of short grasses. In one Kenyan study it fed on the three
       main grasses there, including red oat grass (*Themeda triandra*) and
       finger grass (*Digitaria macroblephara*).
   * - Plains zebra
     - Grasses, including finger grass and oat grass
     - Over 90% of its diet is grass. Red oat grass is one of its staple
       grasses, and in a feeding trial finger grass (*Digitaria*) was its
       favourite of five East African grasses.
   * - Thomson's gazelle
     - Grasses, shoots and herbs
     - Grazes mainly on short grasses, which make up about 90% of its diet
       in the dry season. Red oat grass is named as a preferred grass. It
       prefers the same grasses as wildebeest, so it is linked to finger
       grass as well.
   * - Impala
     - Finger grass, acacia pods and herbs
     - A mixed feeder: mostly grass in the wet season, and leaves and fallen
       acacia pods in the dry season. It prefers soft grasses such as
       finger grass and usually avoids tough red oat grass.
   * - Giraffe
     - Acacia leaves and tender twigs
     - A browser. Acacia leaves and shoots make up most of its diet in most
       areas.
   * - Spotted hyena
     - Wildebeest, zebras, gazelles, impalas and carrion
     - Mostly a hunter (50–90% of its food), taking wildebeest, zebra,
       gazelles and impala.
   * - Cheetah
     - Thomson's gazelles, impalas and young zebras
     - Gazelles and impala are its main prey. In the Serengeti, Thomson's
       gazelles are most of its diet. Zebra foals are taken only now and
       then.
   * - Lion
     - Wildebeest, zebras, gazelles, impalas and giraffes
     - Mainly large grazers and browsers such as wildebeest, zebra and
       giraffe. It also takes impala and gazelles.

.. note::

   This web shows only producers and the animals that hunt or graze. The
   dung beetle card is left out because it is a decomposer: it feeds on dung
   rather than on another organism. Carrion (dead animals) is also left out,
   even though hyenas and lions sometimes eat it.

   .. multichoice::

      Which organisms are the producers in this food web?

      [x] Red oat grass, finger grass and acacia tree | Correct. Producers make their own food from sunlight.
      [ ] Plains zebra, impala and giraffe | Incorrect. These are herbivores: they eat the producers.
      [ ] Lion, cheetah and spotted hyena | Incorrect. These are carnivores at the top of the web.
      [ ] Acacia tree, giraffe and lion | Incorrect. This is a food chain, not the group of producers.

   .. multichoice::

      What does each arrow in a food web show?

      [x] Energy passing from the food to the organism that eats it | Correct. The arrow points to the eater.
      [ ] Energy passing from the eater back to its food | Incorrect. The arrow points from the food to the eater.
      [ ] Which organism is bigger | Incorrect. Arrows show feeding, not size.
      [ ] Where each organism lives | Incorrect. Arrows show feeding, not habitat.

   .. multichoice::

      Which herbivore eats both a grass and the acacia tree?

      [x] Impala | Correct. Impala eat finger grass and acacia pods and leaves.
      [ ] Giraffe | Incorrect. In this web the giraffe only eats the acacia tree.
      [ ] Plains zebra | Incorrect. The zebra only eats grasses.
      [ ] Blue wildebeest | Incorrect. The wildebeest only eats grasses.

   .. multichoice::

      Which animal in this food web eats the giraffe?

      [x] Lion | Correct. The lion is the only animal here that eats giraffes.
      [ ] Cheetah | Incorrect. A giraffe is far too big for a cheetah to hunt.
      [ ] Spotted hyena | Incorrect. There is no arrow from giraffe to spotted hyena in this web.
      [ ] Nothing eats the giraffe | Incorrect. Look for the arrow leaving the giraffe.

   .. multichoice::
      :torf:

      Every food chain in this food web starts with a producer.

      [x] True | Correct. All energy in the web first comes from the producers.
      [ ] False | Incorrect. Every chain starts at red oat grass, finger grass or the acacia tree.

      Which carnivore depends most on Thomson's gazelles for food?

      [x] Cheetah | Correct. Thomson's gazelles are the cheetah's main food.

      [ ] Spotted hyena | Incorrect. Hyenas eat gazelles, but they also eat three other herbivores.

      [ ] Thomson's gazelle | Incorrect. Gazelles are the cheetah's main food.

      Why is the lion called a carnivore?
